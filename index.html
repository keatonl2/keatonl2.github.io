About

A physicist's habits, applied to data

My background is experimental physics. At Hastings College I designed and built a wireless power system with a calculated 82% transfer efficiency for my senior project, and I co-authored a peer-reviewed paper measuring a binary star system with a robotic telescope. That work taught me to treat every number as a measurement: it has a source, an error, and a limit to what it can tell you.

As a graduate student in data science at Bellevue University, I have applied that same discipline to larger and messier data: 1.6 million liquor transactions, 17,000 lottery drawings, a decade of county childcare prices, and insurance records. The projects below are grouped by the kind of evidence they give, from scientific research to predictive modeling and generative AI.

  • Check the data first

    Before modeling Iowa liquor demand, I found and removed 364,000 exact duplicate records that would have taught every model a demand spike that never happened.

  • Test the pattern against chance

    "Hot" lottery numbers look convincing on a chart. Chi-square tests and 1,500 simulated fair draws show they are what randomness produces.

  • Build for the reader

    An infographic for a general audience, a filterable dashboard for policy readers, and slides that explain models in dollars as well as R².

Binary Star System Analysis: WDS 20136+5307

Scientific data analysis and research communication
Published, Journal of Double Star Observations, 2021

I measured six robotic-telescope images in AstroImageJ, cleaned and weighted almost 200 years of historical observations, and compared the result with Gaia satellite data. The question was whether the two stars orbit each other or only line up from Earth.

Data
Las Cumbres Observatory images, U.S. Naval Observatory double-star catalog (1828–2015), Gaia DR2
Tools
AstroImageJ, Plot Tool, error analysis with ±3σ clipping and weighting

Wireless Power Transfer: Designing a Near-Field System

Experimental research and quantitative analysis
Senior physics project, 2022

I built a resonant inductive coupling system from salvaged Qi coils and a custom PWM drive circuit, measured its quality and coupling factors, and mapped how efficiency collapses as the coils separate. I also reviewed why far-field power beaming is still held back by interference, efficiency, and cost.

Data
Inductance, coupling, and oscilloscope measurements from my build, plus coil datasheets and the literature
Tools
Oscilloscope, soldered LC and PWM H-bridge circuits, electromagnetic theory, Qi standard

Chasing Luck: Testing Lottery Results for Patterns

Probability and statistical reasoning
Interactive report

I tested four popular beliefs (hot numbers, cold numbers, overdue numbers, and repeats) against every published Mega Millions, Powerball, and Pick 10 drawing, using chi-square tests and simulated fair draws. The data has patterns, but none of them predict anything.

Data
New York State Open Data: 3,902 jackpot drawings (2002–2024) and 13,570 Pick 10 drawings (1987–2024)
Tools
Chi-square tests, Monte Carlo simulation, kernel density estimation, HTML/JavaScript

Forecasting Store-Level Liquor Demand in Iowa

Predictive analytics and forecasting

Can a retailer's purchase history predict what it will buy over the next four weeks? I first found 364,160 duplicate rows that doubled July and August volume. I then compared three baselines with a Poisson GLM, a random forest, and gradient boosting on held-out weeks. The recommendation: plan at the category and store level with an intermittency-aware baseline, and keep machine learning in shadow mode until it beats that baseline.

Data
Iowa Liquor Sales 2026 (Iowa Department of Revenue): 1.6 million invoice lines, 2,203 stores, 4,429 products
Tools
Python, pandas, scikit-learn (Poisson GLM, random forest, gradient boosting), Croston-SBA, WAPE, time-based validation
